## Artefact 1: Root Cause Analysis Fundamentals

The Business Analyst Job Description (2025) explains Root Cause Analysis as a strategic problem-solving approach. It identifies three RCA areas: safety, production, and business processes. The article emphasises the reactive-to-proactive shift moving from fixing issues after they occur to preventing them beforehand. This distinction is fundamental to understanding RCA's value. The article stresses that real root causes are hidden beneath surface symptoms. If you only fix visible problems, they return. This layered approach to problem analysis is essential for effective process management (The Business Analyst Job Description, 2025, p. 1).

I selected this because it relates to Week 3 lectures identifying Root Cause Analysis as a core process analysis method (ABPMP International, 2019, p. 149). The article demonstrates that RCA represents a strategic mindset shift toward prevention. This shows I understand that process analysis is strategic, not just technical. It requires helping organisations move from "fix problems quickly" to "understand why to prevent recurrence." This understanding of RCA's strategic value demonstrates my learning progression (The Business Analyst Job Description, 2025, p. 2).

## Artefact 2: The 5 Whys and Fishbone Diagram

SGS (2026) compares two distinct RCA methodologies. The 5 Whys technique, developed by Sakichi Toyoda, involves repeated questioning to find root causes. It works best for linear problems. The Fishbone Diagram uses six categories (Man, Machine, Method, Material, Measurement, Milieu) to map multiple causes visually. 

It suits complex problems with multiple factors. The article emphasises that choosing the appropriate tool matters. Each methodology represents different analytical approaches that organisations must understand to select effectively (SGS, 2026, p. 1).

I chose this because it provides practical implementation guidance absent from lectures. Understanding when to apply each technique demonstrates analytical maturity. The 5 Whys requires step-by-step logical thinking, while Fishbone requires systemic mapping. Selecting the wrong tool produces incomplete analysis. This shows I am developing diagnostic capability recognising problem characteristics and selecting appropriate methods. Understanding these complementary techniques across diverse industries (health, manufacturing, IT) shows that effective analysts must master multiple tools (SGS, 2026, p. 2).
