### Artefact 1: Why RCA Matters Strategically
**Source Title**

Root Cause Analysis: Process, Techniques, and Best Practices

**What This Source Says**

This article from The Business Analyst Job Description (2025) explains that Root Cause Analysis is more than just a technical tool. It is actually a way organisations think about problems.

The article makes an important point: RCA can work in three different areas:

•	Safety — stopping workplace accidents

•	Production — making manufacturing faster and better

•	Business processes — improving how work gets done

What I found interesting is that RCA changes how organisations respond to problems. Instead of just fixing the immediate issue (reactive), good organisations use RCA to stop problems before they happen (proactive). That is a big difference. The article explains that real root causes are usually hidden underneath the surface. If you only fix what you see, the problem comes back. You have to dig deeper (The Business Analyst Job Description, 2025, p. 1).

**Why I Chose This**

I picked this because it connects directly to what we learned in Week 3 lectures about how Root Cause Analysis is actually a core part of process analysis (ABPMP International, 2019, p. 149). But this article goes further. It explains why organisations should care about RCA, not just what it is.

The reactive versus proactive thing really clicked for me. In the lectures, we talk about continuous improvement, but this source shows what that actually means in practice. Organisations that are good at process analysis are not just fixing fires. They are preventing them. That is the strategic value.

This is important evidence of my learning because it shows I now understand that process analysis is not just a technical activity. It is about changing how an organisation thinks. A good process analyst needs to help people shift their mindset from "something went wrong, let us fix it quickly" to "something went wrong, let us understand why so it does not happen again." That is much more valuable (The Business Analyst Job Description, 2025, p. 2).

**Reference**

The Business Analyst Job Description (2025). Root Cause Analysis: Process, Techniques, and Best Practices. Available at: https://thebusinessanalystjobdescription.com/root-cause-analysis-steps-techniques-and-best-practices/ (Accessed: 10 August 2026).
